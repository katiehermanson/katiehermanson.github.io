## About Me ##
![Grad Slam](images/gradslam.jpg)
*Katie and Derek at CSU East Bay's Grad Slam 2024.*

### Excerpt from CSU East Bay's Science Stories 2026: ###

Katie Hermanson, a fifth-year Physics and Astronomy major, counts herself among the many dedicated and passionate East Bay undergraduate scholars. Originally starting a career as a social worker with a B.A. in Psychology from the University of Colorado, Katie returned to school after a period of reflection and found deeper satisfaction and purpose in pursuing answers to the largest questions of the cosmos as part of the East Bay Physics community.

Working in the lab of Dr. Derek Jackson Kimball since her first semester, Katie has contributed to a multi-year-long experiment searching for evidence of dark matter in remote wilderness locations. She has also participated in a range of Summer research internships, including studying gravitational wave astronomy at the University of Cardiff and analyzing simluations of dwarf galaxy formation at Carnegie Observatories.

Katie has been involved in numerous endeavours on campus to promote and advocate for science engagment and physics research---she tutored fellow students as a Learning Assistant at the STEMLab, acted as Treasurer for the local Society of Physics Students chapter, served as the American Physical Society's Student Ambassador to East Bay, participated in both LSAMP and East Bay's OSCAR programs, presented her work at Grad Slam and end-of-the-year research showcases, and won first place in the CSU State-wide Student Research competition in 2024. Katie has excelled as a champion of the Physics Department, volunteering for Welcome Days and annual Physics Open Houses and becoming a leader in her research group. For her efforts, she was awarded the Physics Department Outstanding Student of the Year Award and selected to be a Cal-Bridge Undergraduate Scholar in preparation for graduate school.

As a Ph.D. program applicant, Katie was awarded the CSU Sally Casanova Pre-Doctoral Scholarship, representing the most promising students state-wide in their path to graduate studies. Katie is graduating Spring 2027 with a keen eye on computational cosmology research at a top astrophysics graduate program. She hopes to elucidate the most exciting cosmological mysteries regarding the large-scale structure of the universe and the nature of dark matter.
